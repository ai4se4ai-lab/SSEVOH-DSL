The fixed raw-text protocol was a substantial bottleneck: 3 models emitted no syntax-valid output. Invalid, fenced, empty and budget-limited outputs must be distinguished from independently disallowed proposals; rejecting malformed text does not measure policy enforcement. Only 91 of 500 outputs reached the independent admission oracle. The legacy field match scores selected transition fields even for prohibited requests, so it is not a useful deployed-task success rate. The legacy partial field match is preserved in the raw data. A separately labelled post hoc fidelity diagnostic also checks the full snapshot, required evidence, postcondition and applicability of the precondition; neither metric grants deployment permission. Among independently evaluable outputs, 18 were allowed by the reference contract; no false rejection was observed in that subset. These findings apply to the published prompt and decoding budget, not to every way of prompting the models. The deployed metadata control rejected all 60 disallowed fixture trials and retained all 65 allowed trials; its median action time was 0.599 ms. Admission-only baseline timings do not establish an equivalent deployment overhead ratio. The 180 scheduled graph/concurrency trials each retained one committed winner. At 500 components with one contender, median batch times across graph shapes ranged from 0.470 to 0.773 seconds. Traced Python allocations and barrier-synchronized batches are reported separately; they do not establish multi-host throughput or an asymptotic complexity theorem.
